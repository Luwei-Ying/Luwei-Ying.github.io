<section class="content-section shell teaching-intro" aria-labelledby="approach-title">
  <div class="teaching-statement">
    <p class="eyebrow">Approach</p>
    <h2 id="approach-title">Learn the tools.<br>Know when to replace them.</h2>
    <p>Across lectures, simulations, primary sources, and hands-on data work, students learn not only how to use current research tools—including AI—but how to judge when those tools fit, where they fail, and what to learn next.</p>
  </div>

  <ol class="principle-list">
    <li>
      <span>01</span>
      <div>
        <h3>Start from the world</h3>
        <p>Begin with a peace agreement, crisis, institution, or dataset, then use theory to explain what happened.</p>
      </div>
    </li>
    <li>
      <span>02</span>
      <div>
        <h3>Test what you are told</h3>
        <p>Build the habit of asking what evidence would support a claim—and what evidence would change your mind.</p>
      </div>
    </li>
    <li>
      <span>03</span>
      <div>
        <h3>Turn readers into researchers</h3>
        <p>Move from understanding existing work to posing an ambitious question and designing a study that can answer it.</p>
      </div>
    </li>
  </ol>
</section>

  <div class="mentoring-panel">
    <div>
      <p class="eyebrow">Mentoring</p>
      <h2 id="mentoring-title">From a big question to a workable study</h2>
    </div>
    <div>
      <p>I ask students first to articulate the question that genuinely motivates them. We then work backward toward a project they can execute now—grounded in substantive knowledge, an appropriate design, and a willingness to revise.</p>
      <p>Peer feedback and regular deliverables help turn early ideas into research that can travel beyond the classroom.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
